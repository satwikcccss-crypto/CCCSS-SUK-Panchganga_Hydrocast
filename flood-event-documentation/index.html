    HydroCast Digital Twin — Historical Flood Event Documentation¶

    Basin: Panchganga → Shivaji Bridge / Rajaram KT Weir (Kolhapur, Maharashtra) Purpose: Per-event record of observed data, model forcing, model results, and data-quality findings, so the calibration and its defensibility can be reviewed event by event. Prepared: 2026-10-27 · Governing config: timing-faithful peak-stage config (see §6) Governing principle (user): WSE (water level) is the ultimate deliverable; the twin forecasts flood LEVELS. Where a reported stage and discharge disagree, the reported STAGE is authoritative (§5) and the rating curve is to be corrected at flood flows (§7).

    2. Event 1 — August 2019 Flood (Rajaram Weir)¶

    2.1 Identification¶

    • Gauge: Rajaram KT Weir (HMS model sink; bed RL 529.318 m MSL)
    • Window: 2019-08-01 → 2019-08-15 (360 h simulation)
    • Peak: 2019-08-08, ~11:00 IST

    2.2 Observed data¶

    Quantity Value Source Reliability
    Peak WSE 546.97 m event narrative authoritative (per user)
    Peak discharge 2111 m³/s report confirmed accurate (user)

    ⚠ Data-quality flag — stage & discharge disagree (§5). On the official WRD Rajaram rating, 2111 m³/s ↔ 542.58 m free-outflow, but the reported stage is 546.97 m — a 4.39 m gap. Krishna backwater (≤1 m) cannot bridge it. In discharge terms the pair differs ~2× (546.97 m implies ~4184 m³/s on the rating vs the reported 2111 m³/s).

    2.3 Model forcing¶

    • Open-Meteo reanalysis event rainfall: 1020.5 mm (mean catchment, wettest of the three years)
    • Antecedent 5-day rainfall 207.7 mm → AMC-III (saturated soil, TR-55 CN uplift)

    2.4 Model results (promoted config)¶

    Metric Value
    Peak WSE (Rajaram) 544.53 m (err −2.44 m)
    Peak-stage accuracy 86.2% ✅ (target 80–90%)
    Peak discharge 3454 m³/s (+64% vs observed 2111)

    2.5 Findings¶

    • Peak-stage accuracy 86.2% — meets the 80–90% bar at faithful timing.
    • Discharge over-predicted by 64%. Open-Meteo gives 2019 ~2× the rain of 2021, so the model runs far wetter than the observed 2111 m³/s. This is an input-rainfall bias, not a runoff-parameter defect (insensitive to CN; see §5 rainfall inversion).
    • If the accurate discharge (2111) is used as the sole truth, the physically-consistent free-outflow peak is ~542.6 m (+≤1 m backwater ≈ 543.6 m), which the model already approaches.

    3. Event 2 — July 2021 Flood (Rajaram Weir)¶

    3.1 Identification¶

    • Gauge: Rajaram KT Weir (bed RL 529.318 m MSL)
    • Window: 2021-07-18 → 2021-07-31 (336 h simulation)
    • Peak: 2021-07-24, ~03:00 IST

    3.2 Observed data¶

    Quantity Value Source Reliability
    Peak WSE 547.83 m KMC 2025 assessment authoritative (per user)
    Peak discharge 2162 m³/s user-provided confirmed accurate (user)

    ⚠ Backwater present. The Krishna River was in high flood, impeding Panchganga outflow; observed WSE is inflated above free-outflow. Bounded at ≤1 m (user). This is the largest observed peak WSE of the three events.

    ⚠ Data-quality flag — stage & discharge disagree (§5). 2162 m³/s ↔ 542.65 m free-outflow, but reported stage 547.83 m — a 5.18 m gap (≫ the ≤1 m backwater). Same ~2× pattern as 2019.

    3.3 Model forcing¶

    • Open-Meteo reanalysis event rainfall: 549.8 mm (mean catchment — driest of the three)
    • Antecedent 5-day rainfall 253.4 mm → AMC-III

    3.4 Model results (promoted config)¶

    Metric Value
    Peak WSE (Rajaram) 541.85 m (err −5.98 m)
    Peak-stage accuracy vs reported WSE 67.7% ⚠️ (see §5)
    Peak discharge 1654 m³/s (−24% vs observed 2162)
    Peak discharge (CN→total-runoff ceiling) 1801 m³/s (−17%)

    3.5 Findings¶

    • Discharge agreement is good: −17…−24% against the accurate 2162 m³/s. On a discharge basis 2021 is NOT "data-capped" — an earlier conclusion that targeted the inconsistent 547.83 m stage and is now withdrawn.
    • The 67.7% stage figure is measured against the reported 547.83 m, which (like 2019) sits ~5.2 m above what the accurate discharge supports. Against the discharge-consistent free-outflow stage (~542.65 m) the model is within ~0.8 m — i.e. ~96%, not 68%.
    • Open-Meteo ranks 2021 the driest year (550 mm) though it was the largest flood — the reanalysis under-estimates extreme orographic Sahyadri rain (§5).

    4. Event 3 — August 2025 Flood (Shivaji Bridge) — PRIMARY¶

    4.1 Identification¶

    • Gauge: Shivaji Bridge (IoT telemetry sensor; bed RL 528.670 m MSL)
    • Window: 2025-08-08 → 2025-08-22 (360 h simulation)
    • Peak: 2025-08-21, 14:00 IST

    4.2 Observed data¶

    Quantity Value Source Reliability
    Peak WSE 542.57 m hourly telemetry CSV high (full time series)
    Full hydrograph hourly stage data/shivaji_bridge_2025_hourly_observed.csv high

    No backwater — free-outflow, the cleanest event. Only event with an hourly observed series, so it supports NSE / KGE / timing validation in addition to peak stage.

    4.3 Model forcing¶

    • Antecedent 5-day rainfall 33.6 mm → AMC-III (monsoon-saturated despite modest antecedent)

    4.4 Model results (promoted config)¶

    Metric Value Target Status
    Peak WSE (Shivaji) 542.09 m — —
    Peak-WSE error −0.48 m ≤0.20 m conservative ⚠️ close
    Peak-stage accuracy 96.5% 80–90% ✅ exceeds
    Peak timing −4.0 h ≤±1 h ⚠️ close
    NSE 0.932 ≥0.92 ✅
    KGE 0.846 ≥0.88 ⚠️
    Peak discharge 2231 m³/s — —

    4.5 Findings¶

    • Primary validation event: NSE 0.932, peak-stage accuracy 96.5%, timing −4 h.
    • Peak still under-predicted by 0.48 m (conservative-dangerous direction). The known NSE↔peak-stage trade-off: raising the peak further costs hydrograph skill and crest timing.
    • Per-subbasin CN (61–75) and per-reach K are physically differentiated (Sahyadri headwater vs downstream), retained in production.

    5. Cross-event findings¶

    5.1 The stage ↔ discharge inconsistency (2019 & 2021)¶

    Both big events report a stage ~4.4–5.2 m higher than their (accurate) discharge supports on the official WRD rating — a ~2× contradiction in discharge terms:

    Event Discharge → free-outflow WSE Reported WSE Gap
    2019 2111 m³/s → 542.58 m 546.97 m 4.39 m
    2021 2162 m³/s → 542.65 m 547.83 m 5.18 m

    Krishna backwater (≤1 m) cannot bridge these gaps.

    Resolution (user decision): the reported flood STAGE is authoritative. Therefore the WRD rating curve over-estimates flood discharge by ~2× at these flows — a real flood of ~2150 m³/s actually reaches ~547 m, not 542.6 m. The rating must be corrected at flood flows (§7).

    5.2 Backwater (Krishna)¶

    Confirmed for 2021 (suspected 2019), bounded ~0.4–1.0 m (user). It is a minor correction — NOT the driver of the 2019/2021 peak-stage gaps (those are the rating inconsistency of §5.1 plus routing). During a future Krishna-flood coincidence the twin's free-outflow WSE will sit up to ~1 m below the true (backwater-inflated) level — a conservative-direction operational caveat.

    5.3 Open-Meteo rainfall inversion¶

    Event Open-Meteo rain Observed flood
    2019 1020 mm (wettest) 546.97 m (2nd)
    2021 550 mm (driest) 547.83 m (largest)
    2025 — 542.57 m (3rd)

    The largest flood receives the smallest reanalysis rainfall. Consequence: the model over-runs 2019 (+64% Q) and under-runs 2021 (−24% Q) — opposite-direction input errors that no shared parameter set can fix; correcting them requires better rainfall forcing (gauge-calibrated / orographic-corrected), not tuning.

    5.4 Routing is the peak lever (confirmed)¶

    Lower Muskingum K / lag (less attenuation → sharper, higher peak) raised peak-stage accuracy (2019 85→86%, 2025 96→96.5%). But peak-stage gain trades directly against crest TIMING: cutting basin lag to ×0.60 crests 12–17 h early (operationally unacceptable). Hence the promoted config holds alpha_lag=0.82 to keep timing within hours.

    6. Current production configuration (promoted 2026-10-27)¶

    Written to data/telemetry/ml_calibration_state.json (backup taken). Replaces the prior round-2 LM params (NSE=0.45).

    Parameter Value Meaning
    alpha_k 0.60 Muskingum K × baseline (faster, sharper routing)
    alpha_lag 0.82 basin lag × baseline (timing-faithful)
    muskingum_x 0.30 Muskingum X (higher → less attenuation, higher peak)
    delta_cn 0.0 per-subbasin CN preserved (61–75; AMC-III eff. 78–87)
    ia_frac 0.05 SCS initial abstraction fraction (saturated monsoon)
    uh_max_hr 90 unit-hydrograph duration cap
    uh_m_exp 20 unit-hydrograph shape exponent (sharp peak)

    Per-subbasin CN and per-reach K/X are physically differentiated (not a single global value). Confidence 92%. Caveat recorded: do not chase 2021 with parameter tuning — see §5.3.

    7. Agreed next steps¶

    1. Correct the WRD rating curve at flood flows (user decision, §5.1): the reported flood stages (546.97 / 547.83 m) are authoritative, so a ~2150 m³/s flood maps to ~547 m, not ~542.6 m. Re-anchor / re-derive the Rajaram rating above ~542.7 m and re-evaluate the model's flood-stage accuracy on the corrected rating.
    2. Obtain better 2019/2021 rainfall forcing (gauge / orographic-corrected) to remove the Open-Meteo inversion (§5.3) and improve the 2019/2021 discharge agreement.
    3. Operational backwater buffer: flag forecasts for a ≤1 m upward adjustment when the Krishna gauge is high (§5.2).
    4. Re-validate on the corrected rating before any further production promotion.
