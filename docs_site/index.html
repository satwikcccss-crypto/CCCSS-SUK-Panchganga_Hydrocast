HydroCast: Operational Flood Forecasting & Basin Intelligence Continuum

HydroCast is a high-resolution, physics-informed hydrological forecasting and river intelligence platform engineered specifically for the Panchganga River Catchment (1,837.21 km²) in Kolhapur District, Maharashtra, India. It couples automated USACE HEC-HMS 4.13 watershed simulation, ECMWF 9km numerical weather prediction, real-time IoT radar telemetry, and surveyed 2D compound channel hydraulics.

Watershed Physical Description (Simple Engineering Explanation)

The Panchganga river is formed by five major tributaries originating along the high-precipitation crestline of the Western Ghats (Sahyadri Mountains): the Bhogavati, Tulashi, Kumbhi, Dhamani, and Kasari rivers.

Rainfall exceeding 5,000 mm per year falls on the western headwaters (Gaganbawda, Radhanagari), collecting rapidly in steep mountain gorges before draining through the flatter agricultural plains of Kolhapur. The entire basin covers 1,837.21 km² divided into 9 hydrologic subbasins ($S_1 \dots S_9$).

💡 Simple Engineering Explanation for Watershed Delineation:

A watershed (or catchment basin) is an area of land where all the rain that falls drains into a common river outlet. In HEC-HMS, we divide the 1,837 km² Panchganga basin into 9 subbasins along topographic ridgelines. When rain falls in the high western mountains (like Radhanagari S9 or Gaganbawda S6), the water is funneled through the blue river network, converging at Prayag Chikhali before flowing downstream past Shivaji Bridge and Rajaram Weir in Kolhapur.

💡 Simple Engineering Explanation: Why do we subdivide the river channel?

During dry season and early monsoon, river water is confined strictly within the deep rocky/silt main channel. Here, water flows smoothly and fast with low friction (Manning's $n_{\text{main}} = 0.031$).

However, when heavy rains push water above the bankfull level (541.60 m MSL), the river spills over into the flat floodplains. In the Panchganga valley, these floodplains are densely packed with tall standing sugarcane crops and paddy. Sugarcane acts like a thick dense brush, heavily resisting the flow of water (Manning's $n_{\text{flood}} = 0.070$). If engineers calculate the entire cross-section with one single roughness number, the computed flow would be grossly inaccurate. By calculating the main channel and overbank sugarcane sections separately, HydroCast guarantees life-saving accuracy.

💡 Simple Engineering Explanation: Why does water level differ between Shivaji Bridge and Rajaram Weir?

The IoT ultrasonic water level sensor is mounted on Shivaji Maharaj Bridge (Chainage 6+257), while the HEC-HMS model outlet sink is at Rajaram K.T. Weir (Chainage 10+115), which is 3.858 km upstream.

Because water naturally flows downhill along a bed slope of 1:4641, Rajaram's riverbed (529.318m MSL) is physically 0.648 meters higher than Shivaji Bridge's riverbed (528.670m MSL). In addition, during low flow seasons, the solid weir wall at Rajaram (crest RL 530.18m) blocks water for agricultural irrigation, creating an impoundment. HydroCast models this physical drop so that water level measured at Shivaji Bridge accurately informs upstream levels at Rajaram Weir.

💡 Simple Engineering Explanation: Why does Curve Number switch at 65 mm?

Soil acts like a sponge. When rainfall is light or moderate (< 65 mm), the soil is partially dry and soaks up a lot of water before any floods occur (AMC-II).

However, during heavy monsoon downpours where rainfall exceeds 65 mm over 90 hours, the soil pores become completely saturated like a soaked sponge that cannot hold any more water (AMC-III). When this happens, the Curve Number increases dramatically (e.g. from 68 to 83), and almost 100% of any additional rain turns directly into violent river runoff. HydroCast models this saturation switch dynamically to predict sudden flood spikes.

💡 Simple Engineering Explanation: How does Physics-Informed ML prevent false alarms?

Standard "black-box" machine learning algorithms often hallucinate unrealistic flows during extreme events. HydroCast uses Physics-Informed Machine Learning: the ML model is only permitted to adjust physical parameters (such as channel wave speed $K$ and soil retention $CN$) within strict, physically realistic bounds ($0.5 \le \alpha \le 1.8$). If the flood wave is arriving faster than expected, it scales travel time down; if water level is higher, it adjusts soil retention. This guarantees that forecasts remain mathematically and physically sound at all times.
