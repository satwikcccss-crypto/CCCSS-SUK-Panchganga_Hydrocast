## 1. Theoretical Hydraulic Framework

### 1.1 The Classical Manning-Strickler Open-Channel Equation

For uniform steady open channel flow, discharge is governed by Manning's equation:

$$Q = \frac{1}{n} \cdot A \cdot R^{2/3} \cdot S_0^{1/2}$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical Value |
|:---|:---|:---|
| $Q$ | Total river discharge (m³/s) | — |
| $n$ | Composite Manning roughness coefficient | 0.035–0.045 s/m¹ᐟ³ |
| $A$ | Net wetted cross-sectional flow area (m²) | — |
| $P$ | Wetted perimeter along bed and banks (m) | — |
| $R = A/P$ | Hydraulic radius (m) | — |
| $S_0$ | Longitudinal channel energy slope (m/m) | — |

The roughness coefficient reflects gravelly/rocky Deccan trap river beds with seasonal monsoon brush.

## 3. The PBIAS Root Cause & Hydraulic Resolution

The 30% volume bias reported in the first calibration campaign was not a calibration failure. It was a geometry failure: one averaged bed slope across the whole reach, one roughness across channel and floodplain, and a fixed stage subtraction compensating for both. Segmenting the reach, applying the Divided Channel Method and removing the subtraction brought PBIAS to −0.08%.

### 3.1 The Diagnostic Investigation

In early iterations of the system, water stage predictions were reasonably accurate (532.6–533.5 m), but discharge collapsed dramatically to only **16.6 m³/s** (Shivaji) and **10.4 m³/s** (Rajaram), resulting in an unacceptable volumetric percent bias (**PBIAS ≈ 30–40%**).

Three severe hydraulic flaws caused this:

| Flaw | Description | Correction |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **FLAW 1: Unsegmented Bed Slope** | Unsegmented regression against 31 historical high-flood records (stages 542–545.62 m) forced artificial bed slopes: S₀ = 0.0001938 (Shivaji), S₀ = 0.0000767 (Rajaram) | True field-surveyed slopes: S₀ = 0.005858 (Shivaji, 30.2× steeper), S₀ = 0.002318 (Rajaram, 30.2× steeper) |
| **FLAW 2: Wetted Perimeter Discontinuity** | At stage h ≈ 535.5 m, flow spills into wide floodplains. Single cross-section caused wetted perimeter P to jump from 68 m to 310 m while Area A grew slowly. Hydraulic radius R = A/P collapsed from 2.60 m to 1.05 m | **Divided Channel Method** — compute each sub-section separately and sum discharge |
| **FLAW 3: Artificial Offset Hack** | System computed Shivaji rating and subtracted 0.12 m for Rajaram | Rajaram has gentler bed slope (0.002318 vs 0.005858), requiring HIGHER depth for same discharge (backwater pooling). Offset applied per-site with bed datum awareness |

Since velocity $v \propto \sqrt{S_0}$, the artificial slope collapsed velocity from 1.6 m/s down to 0.28 m/s.

### 3.2 The WRD-Anchored PCHIP Hydraulic Calibration (Sep 2026 Correction)

To resolve this, we re-engineered the rating curve engine in [`stage_converter.py`](stage-discharge-conversion.md) (`src/hydrology/stage_converter.py`). Both gauged sites are now **PCHIP-interpolated directly through the official Maharashtra WRD stage-discharge sheet** instead of a pure Manning/DCM curve:

1. **Rajaram K.T. Weir:** PCHIP through the WRD sheet stages verbatim plus WRD 2021–2023 observed low-flow anchors (532.70 → 14.16 … 533.36 → 71.25 m³/s), removing the old fabricated 531.50 → 3.0 tail.

2. **Shivaji Bridge:** PCHIP through the same sheet shifted **−0.648 m downstream** (its bed RL is 0.648 m lower than Rajaram, chainage 6+257 vs 10+115) plus its bed anchor 528.67 → 0. At the same stage Shivaji carries slightly more flow (deeper section). At the same discharge the two curves agree via the offset **above roughly 40 m³/s**, where the shift is exact to within 0.005 m.

   > **Warning:** The offset is not constant at low flow. Below about 40 m³/s each curve is pinned to its own bed level rather than to a shifted copy of the sheet, so the implied offset widens as discharge falls: 0.648 m at 40 m³/s, 0.905 m at 5 m³/s, 1.019 m at 2.8 m³/s and 1.175 m at 1 m³/s. Earlier revisions stated the 0.648 m shift "holds at every anchor", which is not true at the low end.

   This matters because the low-flow end is where the curve is steepest (about 0.63 m of stage per m³/s below 2.8 m³/s, against 0.004 m per m³/s above 1,480 m³/s). Shifting a low stage by 0.648 m and inverting it at the other site compounds two errors and understated the observed flow by more than half. Baseflow is therefore derived directly at the gauged site rather than by transferring stage across the 3.858 km reach. See the [Engineering Autopsy](errors-and-engineering-assumptions.md), item 20.

3. **Monotonic PCHIP Spline Interpolation:** Replaced standard cubic splines with **Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomials (PCHIP)**, which preserves shape and mathematically guarantees strict monotonicity:

$$\frac{dQ}{dh} > 0 \quad \forall h \in [528.67\text{m}, 548.00\text{m}]$$

## 7. Divided Channel Method (DCM) for Sugarcane Overbank Floodplains

To eliminate overbank discharge distortion during high stages (>541.6 m MSL), the flow cross-section is hydraulically subdivided:

| Section | Manning $n$ | Description |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Main Channel | 0.031 | Clean natural riverbed with silt and gravel |
| Overbank Floodplains | 0.070 | Heavy resistance due to dense standing sugarcane crops |

$$Q(H) = \frac{1}{n_{\text{main}}} A_{\text{main}} R_{\text{main}}^{2/3} S_0^{1/2} + \sum_{\text{flood}} \frac{1}{n_{\text{flood}}} A_{\text{flood}} R_{\text{flood}}^{2/3} S_0^{1/2}$$
