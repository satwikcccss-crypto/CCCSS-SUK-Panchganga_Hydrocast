## 1. River Network Geomorphology

The Panchganga River drainage basin exhibits three distinct hydraulic regimes:
1. **Upper Torrential Regime (Radhanagari to Prayag Chikhali):**
   - Distance: $\approx 40\\text{ km}$
   - Bed Slope: **$1:2529$** ($S_0 = 0.000395\\text{ m/m}$)
   - Fast, steep mountain streams carving through volcanic Deccan Trap basalts.
2. **Middle Meandering Regime (Prayag Chikhali to Rajaram K.T. Weir):**
   - Distance: $\approx 18\\text{ km}$
   - Bed Slope: **$1:4641$** ($S_0 = 0.000215\\text{ m/m}$)
   - Broad meandering channel through the Kolhapur urban floodplains. Shivaji Bridge is located at Chainage 6+257, and Rajaram Weir at Chainage 10+115.
3. **Lower Flat Backwater Regime (Rajaram K.T. Weir to Shirol K.T. Weir):**
   - Distance: $\approx 42\\text{ km}$
   - Bed Slope: **$1:7700$** ($S_0 = 0.000130\\text{ m/m}$)
   - Extremely flat hydraulic gradient subject to massive backwater effects from the Krishna River confluence during concurrent high flows.

## 2. Kolhapur Type (K.T.) Weirs & Backwater Dynamics

The Panchganga river system contains numerous Kolhapur Type (K.T.) weirs built for post-monsoon agricultural irrigation storage:
- **Structure:** Low-height masonry/concrete barrages with vertical steel needle gates.
- **Monsoon Operation:** During the peak monsoon (June to September), needle gates are fully removed to allow unrestricted passage of flood waves.
- **Post-Monsoon / Summer Operation:** Needle gates are reinstalled, raising water levels to the crest level ($530.18\\text{ m}$ MSL at Rajaram Weir) to impound irrigation water.
- **Hydraulic Backwater:** HydroCast incorporates the backwater curve of the weir in its low-flow stage-discharge ratings.
